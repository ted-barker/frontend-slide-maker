The Core Question

Surveys start with populations, not questionnaires

If we don't think carefully about who is in the population, who is reachable, and who needs to be analyzable, we can end up with a large dataset that still fails the research question.

🔍

What Surveys Are Good For

How Much.
How Often.
How Many.
How Different.

Surveys are strongest at measurement

  • Prevalence
  • Comparing groups
  • Tracking change
  • Turning behavior into quantitative data

What Surveys Are Not Good For

Surveys are not the best tool for depth, discovery, or causal explanation

Also not ideal when groups are:

  • Small
  • Uneven
  • Hard to identify

Why This Matters in Tech

The "known" population is often less informative than the unknown one

What you know:
  • Account tier
  • Region
  • Product usage
  • Company size
  • Tenure
What matters more:
  • Willingness to respond
  • Technical sophistication
  • Decision authority
  • Churn risk
  • List representativeness

Probabilistic Sampling

Each person has a known, non-zero chance of selection

This gives the survey a defensible basis for inference

Three sampling approaches:

1. Random sampling
Simple, but often not practical
2. Stratified sampling
Divide first, then sample within groups
3. Oversampling
Sample rare groups at higher rates
