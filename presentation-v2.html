Surveys measure scale.
They don't explain behaviour.

Surveys answer "how many, how often, how different." They don't answer "why." That boundary isn't a limitation to apologise for, it's a design constraint to respect.

The real work starts
before the questionnaire.

Survey design begins with the population, not the questions. Who is in the population? Who is reachable? Who needs to be analyzable? Getting these wrong produces a large dataset that fails the research question.

Your sample can be too thin
for the question you're actually asking.

1,000 responses sounds like a lot until you slice it by five segments. The relevant N drops fast. Subgroup analysis needs its own power calculation, not a share of the overall total.

Rare groups need oversampling
or they disappear.

If a segment is business-critical but small, you sample it at a higher rate than its population share. Then you weight back at analysis. This isn't a workaround, it's standard practice for hard-to-reach populations.

The list you can see
isn't the population you can claim.

The operational population (CRM, account list) and the analytic population (who you can actually make claims about) are not the same thing. The gap between them is where survey error lives.
